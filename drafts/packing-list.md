# Packing list and documents

## Climate-appropriate items

- **Light, breathable clothes.** The mean annual temperature is about 26.6°C, and humidity is high all year. ([PAGASA](https://www.pagasa.dost.gov.ph/information/climate-philippines))
- **Rain gear for June–November,** the rainy season. ([PAGASA](https://www.pagasa.dost.gov.ph/information/climate-philippines))
- **A layer for Baguio.** It has its own cooler climate, which PAGASA excludes from the national temperature average. ([PAGASA](https://www.pagasa.dost.gov.ph/information/climate-philippines))
- **A refillable bottle or water filter.** Tap water isn't safe to drink anywhere in the country. Choose sealed bottled water and consider skipping ice. ([US State Dept](https://travel.state.gov/content/travel/en/traveladvisories/traveladvisories/philippines-travel-advisory.html))

## Power and adapters

- **Voltage:** 220–230V at 60Hz. ([US State Dept](https://travel.state.gov/content/travel/en/traveladvisories/traveladvisories/philippines-travel-advisory.html); [WorldStandards](https://www.worldstandards.eu/electricity/plug-voltage-by-country/philippines/))
- **Outlets look like US outlets but run on 220V.** Check that each device is rated for it. Labels reading "100–240V" are fine. ([US State Dept](https://travel.state.gov/content/travel/en/traveladvisories/traveladvisories/philippines-travel-advisory.html))
- **Plug types:** A, B, and C. Most sockets are hybrids that accept both A and C. ([WorldStandards](https://www.worldstandards.eu/electricity/plug-voltage-by-country/philippines/))

## Medicines

- **Keep prescription meds in their original packaging** with your doctor's prescription, and check with the Bureau of Customs that each one is legal. ([US State Dept](https://travel.state.gov/content/travel/en/traveladvisories/traveladvisories/philippines-travel-advisory.html))
- **No cannabis in any form,** including CBD, edibles, and vape cartridges. It is illegal even with a US prescription and can bring drug-trafficking charges. ([US State Dept](https://travel.state.gov/content/travel/en/traveladvisories/traveladvisories/philippines-travel-advisory.html))
- **Health:** Check [CDC Travelers' Health](https://wwwnc.cdc.gov/travel/destinations/traveler/none/philippines). Most US health plans don't cover care abroad, so consider medical-evacuation insurance. ([US State Dept](https://travel.state.gov/content/travel/en/traveladvisories/traveladvisories/philippines-travel-advisory.html))

## Documents

- **Passport:** US visitors need 6 months' validity past arrival. No visa is needed for stays of 30 days or less. ([US State Dept](https://travel.state.gov/content/travel/en/traveladvisories/traveladvisories/philippines-travel-advisory.html))
- **eTravel QR code**, registered within 72 hours before arrival. ([eTravel FAQ](https://etravel.gov.ph/en/frequently-asked-questions))
- **Balikbayan privilege (RA 6768 as amended by RA 9174):**
  - **Who qualifies:**
    - Filipinos continuously abroad for at least 1 year
    - OFWs
    - Former Filipinos naturalized abroad
    - Their spouse and children, if they are nationals of visa-free (EO 408) countries **and travel together** with the balikbayan
  - **Privileges:**
    - **One-year visa-free entry** for foreign-passport holders
    - US$1,500 tax-exempt purchases at government duty-free shops, plus a US$2,000 Kabuhayan livelihood-tools allowance
    - Travel tax exemption
  - **Bring:** A passport valid 6 months past your intended stay, a round-trip ticket, and **proof of former Philippine citizenship** (old PH passport or PSA birth certificate).
  - ([Philippine Embassy New Zealand: Balikbayan Program FAQ](https://www.philembassy.org.nz/faq/on-the-balikbayan-program))
- **Dual citizenship (RA 9225):**
  - **Who can apply:** Former natural-born Filipinos naturalized abroad.
  - **How:** Apply at the BI or a Philippine consulate. Steps include document pre-screening, the Oath of Allegiance, and paying fees. You then receive the Certificate of Retention/Re-acquisition, Order of Approval, and Oath.
  - **Fees at the BI in Manila:** P3,010 total (fees "updated as of 06 March 2014").
  - ([Bureau of Immigration](https://immigration.gov.ph/services/application-for-retention-re-acquisition-of-phil-citizenship/))
  - **Tip:** In the US, your consulate's page lists local requirements, for example [PCG Los Angeles](https://losangelespcg.org/consular-services-2/dual-citizensh1p-ra-9225/).
  - **UNVERIFIED:** Consulate fees in US dollars.
- **Traveling with kids:** Non-Filipino children under 15 arriving without a parent need a Waiver of Exclusion Ground. ([US State Dept](https://travel.state.gov/content/travel/en/traveladvisories/traveladvisories/philippines-travel-advisory.html))
- **Long stays:** Staying more than 6 months requires an Emigration Clearance Certificate before you leave. ([US State Dept](https://travel.state.gov/content/travel/en/traveladvisories/traveladvisories/philippines-travel-advisory.html))
- **Safety:** The US advisory is **Level 2, Exercise increased caution** (issued Sep 1, 2026).
  - **Level 4 (Do not travel):** The Sulu Archipelago and Marawi City.
  - **Level 3 (Reconsider travel):** Most of Mindanao, with listed exceptions such as Davao City CBD, Siargao, and Samal Island.
  - Enroll in STEP before you go.
  - ([US State Dept](https://travel.state.gov/content/travel/en/traveladvisories/traveladvisories/philippines-travel-advisory.html))

## Balikbayan boxes

- **Duty- and tax-free if** the box is worth **P150,000 or less**, holds only personal and household effects, and isn't in commercial quantities.
- **Limit:** **3 times per calendar year.** Beyond that, duties apply.
- **Documents:** An Information Sheet, a copy of your passport bio page, and an invoice or receipt if you have one. Submit them through the consolidator abroad.
- **Use a DTI-accredited forwarder** (listed at dti.gov.ph) and declare items and values correctly.
- ([Bureau of Customs: Guidelines on Balikbayan Boxes, CAO 01-2018](https://customs.gov.ph/boc-guidelines-on-balikbayan-boxes-2/))
- **Separate rule for luggage:** Returning balikbayans have their own duty-free allowance for personal effects, with thresholds that depend on time spent abroad. ([Bureau of Customs: duty and tax free privileges](https://customs.gov.ph/duty-and-tax-free-privileges/))
